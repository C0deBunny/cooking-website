You asked about full-bleed — here is what you actually picked

"D3" only meant the title sits on the photo rather than in a tinted band beneath it. How wide the photo goes was the separate question, and D3-a caps it at the reading column — so it is not edge to edge. All three keep your 1:1 crop intact; they differ only in how much width the hero claims. Drag the viewport to compare, then tell me if (a) is still the one.

not full-bleed

Identical at every width, so the rail and the page agree with no breakpoint.

D3-c — edge to edge on mobile, capped above

One rule: w-full md:max-w-3xl md:mx-auto.

full-bleed where it is cheap

Genuinely edge to edge on phones, capped where a square would take over the screen. One layout, no blur, no second image.

The hero is only ever a square, so the crop guarantee holds at every breakpoint.

Two visual behaviours to hold in your head, and the rail is always in capped mode — the mobile look is never previewed.

Why none of them is "1:1 spanning the viewport": a square that wide is that tall. At 1400px the hero would be 1400px before the title, so nothing else is ever above the fold. That option only exists if you give up either the square (back to a wide crop, and the card re-crops it unpreviewed) or the crop guarantee (max-h + object-cover, which re-crops what you framed).

This is where D3 bites, and my earlier version of this section drew it wrong — it put the cover below the title, which is D2. Under D3 the title lives on the photo, so "no cover" means the header has to be something else entirely. That makes it a bigger question than a missing box: the whole header swaps layout when a photo lands.
